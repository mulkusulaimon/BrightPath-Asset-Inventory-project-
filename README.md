# BrightPath-Asset-Inventory-project-
GRC Project: IT Asset Inventory and Security Risk Identification

Project Type: Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)
Level: Entry-Level / Junior GRC Analyst
Organization: BrightPath Digital Services Ltd. (Fictional)
Framework Reference: NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0
Project Status: Completed as a learning exercise; validation of assumptions remains outstanding.

1. Project Overview

This project demonstrates how a junior GRC analyst can document an organization’s important assets, identify ownership responsibilities, classify information, and record potential security risks.

The scenario involves a fictional digital marketing company with 15 employees. The organization uses company laptops, corporate email, cloud-based customer information, accounting software, a company website, and privileged accounts.

The exercise focuses on establishing a structured asset inventory that could support risk assessments, access reviews, and broader security governance activities.

2. Business Problem

The fictional organization needs a structured record of its important assets and the information they contain.

Without an organized inventory, management may struggle to determine:

* Which assets need protection.
* Who is accountable for each asset.
* Who manages the asset’s technical security.
* What information requires additional protection.
* Which security concerns require further investigation.
* What evidence is needed to establish whether security controls are operating effectively.

3. Project Objectives

The objectives were to:

1. Identify important hardware, software, information, services, and accounts.
2. Assign proposed business owners and technical custodians.
3. Identify the information associated with each asset.
4. Apply appropriate information classification concepts.
5. Document potential security concerns.
6. Distinguish existing controls from proposed or unverified controls.
7. Map relevant activities to NIST CSF 2.0 categories.

4. Deliverable

The primary deliverable is an Excel-based asset inventory.

It includes:

* Asset ID and name.
* Asset category.
* Business owner.
* Technical custodian.
* Information stored or business function.
* Information classification.
* Security concern.
* Control status and existing-control information.
* Review status.
* Indicative NIST CSF 2.0 references.
* Learning notes explaining key decisions.

The workbook also includes a learning guide and interview preparation notes.

5. Key GRC Findings and Recommendations

A. Asset ownership and accountability

Business ownership and technical custody are different responsibilities.

For example, a Customer Operations Manager may be accountable for customer information, while the IT Manager manages technical security and access controls.

The proposed assignments in this exercise require validation against the fictional organization’s actual responsibilities.

B. Information classification

Information should be classified according to its sensitivity and the organization’s classification policy.

Public website content may be classified as Public, while customer records may require Confidential or Restricted classification.

A system can contain information with different classifications, so the classification should not automatically be applied to the entire system.

C. Shared administrator account

The scenario identifies a shared administrator account as a security concern.

Shared privileged credentials can make it difficult to attribute actions to individual users and increase the potential impact of compromised credentials.

Recommended actions include:

* Replacing routine shared administrator access with individually assigned accounts.
* Restricting administrative privileges to authorized personnel.
* Enabling appropriate activity logging.
* Conducting periodic privileged-access reviews.

D. Control verification

A control listed in a spreadsheet is not necessarily implemented or effective.

Controls must be verified through appropriate evidence, such as configuration records, access review results, or relevant system logs.

For this fictional exercise, controls that have not been established by the scenario are labelled as proposed or unverified.

6. Framework Mapping

The inventory uses indicative references to NIST CSF 2.0, including:

* ID.AM — Asset Management: Identifying and managing organizational assets.
* PR.AA — Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control: Supporting appropriate access management.
* PR.DS — Data Security: Supporting the protection of information.
* DE.CM — Continuous Monitoring: Relevant to monitoring activities involving systems and accounts.

These references help connect the inventory to cybersecurity risk management outcomes. They do not establish that the fictional organization complies with NIST CSF 2.0.

Framework reference: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework

7. Skills Demonstrated

This project demonstrates practice in:

* Asset inventory documentation.
* Business ownership and technical custody.
* Information classification.
* Security risk identification.
* Access-control risk analysis.
* Control verification and evidence awareness.
* NIST CSF 2.0 mapping.
* GRC documentation and reporting.

8. Limitations

BrightPath Digital Services Ltd. is a fictional organization created for learning purposes.

The project is not based on a real client engagement, independent audit, or verified organizational assessment.

Asset ownership assignments and control assumptions require validation within the scenario. No claim of organizational compliance, certification, or control effectiveness is made.

9. Key Learning Outcome

The main lesson from this exercise is that an asset inventory is more than a list of devices and applications.

It provides a foundation for understanding accountability, information sensitivity, security concerns, and the evidence needed to manage risk.
